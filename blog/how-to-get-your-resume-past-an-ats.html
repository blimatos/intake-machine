How to get your resume past an ATS

Forty applications and no replies is rarely about your experience. Most of the time, a few missing words decide it before anyone reads a line.

BBy Bruno, co-founderPublished Nov 3, 2026Updated Nov 3, 20262 min read

The short answer

Most resumes are filtered by the applicant tracking system because they miss words from the posting or use a layout the software cannot read. Fix both: use a one-column template, score your resume against each posting, and add the missing keywords that are true for you. It takes about twenty minutes per job.

  • Use a one-column template with standard headings.
  • Score against the real posting, not a generic list.
  • Add the missing words only where they are true.
  • Re-score until you clear the line, then apply.

Do it in five steps

  1. 1Pick a readable templateOne column, standard headings, no tables or text boxes. About 2 minutes.
  2. 2Paste the real postingScore against this job. The words that matter change every time. About 2 minutes.
  3. 3Add the missing words that are trueIn your summary and bullets. Never claim a skill you do not have. About 10 minutes.
  4. 4Match the titleUse the title the posting searches for, next to yours: "Program Lead (Senior Program Manager scope)". About 2 minutes.
  5. 5Re-score, then sendEdit until you clear the line. Keep the version, named for the job. About 4 minutes.

What does an ATS actually do?

An applicant tracking system is the software employers use to collect applications. It reads your resume into fields (name, titles, dates, skills) and ranks it against the posting. Recruiters usually start at the top of that ranking. If your resume ranks low, or the software cannot read it, a person may never open it.

Why is my resume not getting through?

Three reasons, in the order we see them most:

  • Missing words. The posting says "stakeholder management"; your resume says "worked with partners".
  • Layout. Two columns, tables and text boxes get read out of order.
  • Titles. Your title at your company is not the title the posting searches for.

Does tailoring really make a difference?

Yes. In Huntr's data for early 2026, applications sent with a resume tailored to the posting led to an interview 4.23% of the time, against 2.07% with an untailored resume. That is about twice the rate, across 139,927 applications. Tailoring is not rewriting from scratch. It is the summary, the top bullets and the words the posting uses.

Questions people ask

Does every company use an ATS?

Many do, especially larger employers. A clean, keyword-matched resume works whether a person or software reads it first.

Can I beat it with hidden white text?

No. Recruiters see the plain text, so hidden keywords show up, and they read as a trick.

PDF or Word file?

Follow the posting. If it does not say, a text-based PDF or a .docx both read fine. A scanned PDF does not.

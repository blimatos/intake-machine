What does an ATS do with your resume?

An ATS, or applicant tracking system, is the software employers use to collect and sort applications. When you apply, it reads your resume and files what it finds into a candidate profile: your name, contact details, job titles, dates and skills. Recruiters then search those profiles by keyword, the way you would search a shopping site, and start with the people at the top of the list.

Almost every large employer works this way. Jobscan found an ATS behind the careers pages of 487 of the Fortune 500 in 2026 (Jobscan, 2026). In its survey of recruiters, 99.7% said they use keyword filters in their ATS to sort applicants (Jobscan, 2026).

That is part of why so many applications go quiet. For every 100 people who apply to a posting, about three are invited to interview (CareerPlug, 2025). If the software misreads your resume, or never finds the words the recruiter types, you are not one of the three.

What makes a resume ATS-friendly?

You can do this by hand, in any word processor. Two things decide whether the software gets your resume right: a layout it reads whole, and the words the job uses.

A layout it reads whole

  • One column. Text runs top to bottom, with no side bar, table or text box. Software reads columns in an order you can't control.
  • Standard section names. Summary, Skills, Experience, Education. The software looks for them to know where each part starts.
  • Your name and contact details in the page, not in Word's header or footer.
  • Each job in the same pattern. Title, company, place and dates together, with dates as month and year.
  • Skills as words, never as bars, stars or icons. No photo.
  • A Word file (.docx), or a PDF saved from it. Never a scan.

Layout changes what lands where. In a 2026 test of 357 runs through seven text-extraction tools, the words came through from both layouts, but sections broke apart more often in two columns. With the weakest reader, sections stayed whole 35% of the time with two columns, against 60% with one (Enhancv, 2026). A title that lands away from its dates, or skills that land in the wrong field, are words a recruiter's search never finds.

The words the job uses

Recruiters search with the words in their posting. Use the posting's job title at the top of your resume when it is true for you: in Jobscan's analysis of nearly one million job searches, resumes with the job title got 10.6 times more interview invitations (Jobscan, 2026). Then add the skills and terms the posting repeats, in its own wording, wherever they are true: your summary, your skills and your bullets.

This part changes with every job, so build the resume once and tailor it each time. It pays. In Huntr's data for early 2026, applications with a resume tailored to the posting led to an interview 4.23% of the time, against 2.07% without, across 139,927 applications (Huntr, Q1 2026).

Mistakes to avoid

  • Two columns, tables or text boxes. Software can read them out of order, or skip them.
  • Contact details in Word's header or footer. Some systems never read them. Type them in the page.
  • Skill bars, icons and photos. A bar is a picture of a skill, and nobody can search a picture. Write skills as words.
  • Creative section names. "Where I've made an impact" is not a heading software expects. Use Summary, Skills, Experience and Education.
  • Pasting with the old formatting. The old styles come along with the words. Paste as plain text.
  • One resume for every job. Build it once, then tailor the summary, skills and top bullets to each posting.
  • A comma in your job title. "Director, Commercial Banking" can be misread. Write it the way postings and recruiters do: "Director of Commercial Banking".
  • Keywords you can't back up. If it is on your resume, expect to be asked about it.

Questions people ask

Do I need a BOLDr Careers account?

Yes. ATS Resume Template is part of BOLDr Careers. The account is free: get your free read, choose the Free plan, and both templates are ready to download.

Does every company use an ATS?

Almost every large one: Jobscan found an ATS at 487 of the Fortune 500 in 2026. Many smaller employers use one too, through their job board or hiring software. A clean one-column resume works either way, whether software or a person reads it first.

Word or PDF?

Keep Word as your master copy. Send what the posting asks for. If it doesn't say, a Word file or a PDF saved from it both work, because a PDF saved from Word keeps real text. A scanned PDF does not.

One page or two?

Two is normal after about ten years. Either way, your strongest proof goes in the top half of page one.

Can I change the font or color?

Yes. Any common font, body text at 10 to 12 points, and one color for headings. Keep the one column and the section names.

Can I beat an ATS with hidden white text?

No. The ATS turns your resume into plain text, so hidden words show up for the recruiter, and they read as a trick.
